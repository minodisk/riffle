### Findings

1. **crates/core/src/eyes.rs:177-178**: `Judged.pose` is still documented as "`None` when the solve fails", but with this PR `head_pose` also returns `None` for a fit whose roll is beyond `MAX_ROLL` (a successful solve that is rejected as implausible). 2. **docs/plans/20261007-head-pose/plan.md:357-361**: The Decision says the EXIF FOV and weak-perspective variants are "no better on the classes" and cites only yaw (107 / 105 against 108). But `pose-results.md` shows both doing better on the roll class (145/159 against 142/159) and worse on the pitch class (124 / 122 against 125). The conclusion still holds (a 3-face gain on roll is within label noise, and pitch moves the other way), but the sentence as written misstates the table. Say the variants trade a few faces between the roll and pitch classes and win nothing overall, or cite all three classes.
3. **docs/plans/20261007-head-pose/plan.md:371-372**: "the sign is right on about 94% of turned and tilted faces" is meant to go into the Step 3/4 docs, but 94% is the yaw (93.8%) and pitch (94.8%) figure. The roll sign, which a reader will most naturally call "tilted", is 15/18 (83.3%) in `pose-results.md`. Name the axes, e.g. "about 94% for yaw and pitch, 15 of 18 for roll", so the user-facing number does not overstate roll.
4. **docs/plans/20261007-head-pose/pose-results.md:115-117**: The roll misses are explained as "two of them DNG portraits turned about 30 deg". However, every DNG face labeled roll `left` in `pose-truth.md` (#12, #28, #171, #183) is labeled yaw `frontal` (under ~15 deg). If "turned about 30 deg" is the measured yaw, say so (e.g. "that the fit reads as turned about 30 deg though labeled frontal"). Otherwise the explanation contradicts the labels it is measured against.
